A weekly catch-up on infectious disease research, methods and policy

Issue 001 · 17 October 2026 · Pre-release draft

RIDA Radar is an AI-assisted, human-guided selection of developments in infectious disease epidemiology, modelling, methods and public health policy. Sources are monitored and items shortlisted based on predefined editorial criteria, with final selection and review by RIDA.

Originally developed as a way to keep up with developments in a rapidly evolving field, RIDA Radar shares selected findings that may also be useful or interesting to others.

It offers a selective weekly catch-up, rather than a comprehensive overview of everything published.

The week in 30 seconds

Three developments selected for their relevance, practical value or potential interest according to RIDA Radar's editorial criteria—not an objective ranking of the week's most important news.

  • Epidemic intelligence is evolving. WHO's new 2026–2030 Pandemic Hub strategy sets an ambitious goal of detecting emerging outbreaks within 24 hours.
  • The criteria for adding diseases to the WHO NTD list are under review. Requests for additions have been temporarily paused while the existing criteria are reassessed.
  • NTD medicine supply chains remain an implementation priority. WHO and partners are addressing forecasting, logistics, inventory visibility and country-level coordination.

In case you missed it

WHO Pandemic Hub sets an ambitious goal for outbreak detection

12 October 2026 · WHO · Epidemic intelligence

WHO announced its 2026–2030 strategy for pandemic and epidemic intelligence, setting an ambition to enable detection of new outbreaks within 24 hours. The strategy considers surveillance, open-source epidemic intelligence, modelling, genomics and digital tools. This is a stated goal rather than an achieved outcome.

Why it matters: Connecting multiple epidemiological information streams to analytical methods raises important questions about interoperability, data quality, validation and how intelligence supports public health decision-making.

Suggested action: Read the strategy announcement (around five minutes).

Source: WHO — Pandemic Hub announcement

WHO pauses requests to include new neglected tropical diseases

15 October 2026 · WHO · NTD policy

WHO reported a recommendation to pause requests to add new diseases to its NTD list while the inclusion criteria, which date from 2016, are reviewed.

Why it matters: How a disease is classified can affect its visibility and the framing of research and programme priorities. The review is also relevant to discussions of epidemiological evidence and burden of disease in policy decisions.

Suggested action: Read the WHO update (around three minutes).

Source: WHO — NTD inclusion criteria

NTD medicine supply chains: From forecasting to delivery

13 October 2026 · WHO · Programme implementation

WHO and partners reported on efforts to strengthen NTD medicine supply chains. The update covers forecasting, supply planning, inventory visibility and coordination with national delivery systems, including progress on standard operating procedures in priority countries.

Why it matters: Quantitative forecasts alone do not ensure that medicines reach the populations that need them. Planning has to remain aligned with programme needs, supply constraints and implementation capacity.

Suggested action: Read for an example of evidence-to-implementation challenges (around five minutes).

Source: WHO — Supply chain coordination

MERS-CoV: WHO reports cases from Saudi Arabia

15 October 2026 · WHO Disease Outbreak News · Surveillance

WHO published an update on Middle East respiratory syndrome coronavirus (MERS-CoV) in Saudi Arabia, reporting eight laboratory-confirmed cases and one death during January to early October 2026.

Why it matters: This is a useful surveillance update for tracking zoonotic infectious disease risks and considering how to interpret small numbers of reported cases in context.

Suggested action: Skim for situational awareness.

Source: WHO — MERS-CoV outbreak update

Worth a closer look

The following items were published earlier in October and are included for their methodological or practical relevance.

Global dengue update: Surveillance and trends in 2026

9 October 2026 · WHO Weekly Epidemiological Record

WHO published an update on global dengue surveillance and reported activity during 2026, including data through August.

Why it matters: Global comparisons depend on differences in surveillance practices, reporting completeness and the integration of different data sources. This is relevant to analyses of vector-borne disease burden and epidemiological trends.

Suggested action: Read and explore the surveillance methods.

Source: WHO — Global dengue update

Beyond coverage: How do we measure health campaign effectiveness?

7 October 2026 · Health Campaign Effectiveness Coalition

A webinar recording introduces a framework for assessing the effectiveness of health campaigns beyond conventional coverage indicators, with examples from vitamin A supplementation programmes.

Why it matters: Coverage is an important programme measure, but it does not by itself capture equity, implementation quality, efficiency or the full consequences of a campaign. The framework could offer useful perspectives for vaccine, malaria and NTD intervention assessment.

Suggested action: Watch or save the framework for closer examination.

Source: Campaign Effectiveness — Beyond Coverage

Onchocerciasis elimination: Progress during 2025–2026

9 October 2026 · WHO Weekly Epidemiological Record

WHO published a progress report on the elimination of human onchocerciasis, including preventive chemotherapy and progress towards transmission interruption.

Why it matters: Elimination assessments depend on surveillance indicators, programme data quality and evidence for interruption of transmission—questions relevant to modelling and NTD programme evaluation.

Suggested action: Bookmark for NTD reading.

Source: WHO — Onchocerciasis progress report

Something to explore

WHO Hub for Pandemic and Epidemic Intelligence

The WHO Pandemic Hub supports collaborative approaches to epidemic intelligence, surveillance, data analysis and decision-making.

Why it matters: Its work offers a useful case study in integrating data, analytical methods and AI while maintaining source evaluation and human oversight. This is an established initiative highlighted by a new strategy, not a newly released software tool.

Suggested action: Explore when time permits.

Source: WHO Pandemic Hub
